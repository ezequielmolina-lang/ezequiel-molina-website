## Flagship Programs Created by Ezequiel Molina

### Teach - Classroom Observation Tool

**What is Teach?**
Teach is a free, open-source classroom observation tool designed to help countries track and improve teaching quality in primary, early childhood, and secondary education settings. It was developed by the World Bank under Ezequiel Molina's leadership.

**Key Features:**
- Measures both the time teachers spend on learning and the quality of teaching practices
- Captures practices that enhance students' cognitive and socio-emotional skills
- Uses the Universal Design for Learning Framework
- Provides standardized metrics across different contexts and countries

**Global Reach:**
- Adopted by 40+ countries worldwide
- Used by UNESCO, UNICEF, and multiple NGOs
- Reached 180,000+ teachers
- Impacted 3.6+ million students
- Countries include: Afghanistan, Jordan, Mongolia, Peru, Pakistan (Punjab), Rwanda, Tanzania, Uruguay, and many others

**Validation Research:**
The Teach tool has been rigorously validated. Results from Punjab, Pakistan showed:
- Scores were internally consistent
- Good inter-rater reliability
- Sufficient information to differentiate low from high-quality teaching practices
- Higher Teach scores were associated with higher student outcomes in language and mathematics

### Coach - Teacher Professional Development

**What is Coach?**
Coach is the World Bank's global initiative that helps countries improve in-service teacher professional development systems to accelerate student learning. It incorporates insights from adult learning and behavioral science.

**Key Features:**
- Evidence-based approach to teacher coaching
- Behavioral science insights for sustained behavior change
- Practical tools for classroom implementation
- Scalable model for different contexts

**Funding and Reach:**
- Secured $7M+ in funding
- Operating in 15+ countries
- Designed to complement the Teach observation tool

### El Director LIBRE (The Liberated Principal)

**What is El Director LIBRE?**
A practical method that uses artificial intelligence ethically and responsibly to help school leaders reduce paperwork and improve instructional leadership. The method translates decades of research into simple AI-powered tools that free up time to lead learning and improve student outcomes.

**Impact:**
- 2,000+ school directors trained in Peru
- 13,000+ downloads since November 2025 launch
- RCT (Randomized Controlled Trial) underway with ~500 teachers showing sustained adoption
- Being scaled across Latin America with adaptation to local contexts

**Partnerships:**
- Collaboration with Fundacion Romero (Peru's largest bank's foundation)
- Microsoft partnership
- UPC university partnership
- Inter-American Development Bank coordination

### Peer-Reviewed Journal Articles

#### 6. Teaching for all? Measuring the quality of inclusive practices across eight countries (2024)
**Authors:** Molina, E., Carter, E., Luna Bazaldua, D., Pushparatnam, A., & Singal, N.
**Journal:** Compare: A Journal of Comparative and International Education, 54(4), 641-659

**Abstract:**
While consensus exists on the importance of inclusive education, there is limited evidence on the use and quality of inclusive teaching practices. This article uses the Universal Design for Learning Framework and Teach observation tool to examine use of inclusive teaching practices across eight countries (Afghanistan, Jordan, Mongolia, Peru, Pakistan Punjab, Rwanda, Tanzania, and Uruguay) and their relationship to other teaching quality behaviors.

#### 7. Evidence-based teaching: effective teaching practices in primary school classrooms (2024)
**Authors:** Carter, E., Molina, E., Pushparatnam, A., Rimm-Kaufman, S., Tsapali, M., & Wong, K.K.-Y.
**Journal:** London Review of Education, 22(1), 8

**Abstract:**
This article addresses understanding and measuring effective teaching practices in low- and middle-income countries using the Teach Primary framework. It examines evidence regarding effective instructional practices in primary school classrooms, with highlighted examples from developing countries.

#### 8. Measuring the quality of teaching practices in primary schools: Assessing the validity of the Teach observation tool in Punjab, Pakistan (2020)
**Authors:** Molina, E., Fatima, S.F., Ho, A.D., Melo, C., Wilichowski, T.M., & Pushparatnam, A.
**Journal:** Teaching and Teacher Education, 96, 103171

**Abstract:**
This paper evaluates the validity of Teach using data from Punjab, Pakistan. Results show that Teach scores were internally consistent, presented good inter-rater reliability, and provided sufficient information to differentiate low from high-quality teaching practices. Higher Teach scores were associated with higher student outcomes.

#### 9. The impact of strengthening agricultural extension services on women farmers: Evidence from Ethiopia (2019)
**Authors:** Buehren, N., Goldstein, M., Molina, E., & Vaillant, J.
**Journal:** Agricultural Economics, 50(4), 407-419

**Abstract:**
This study examines the effects of strengthening agricultural extension services on women farmers in Ethiopia, demonstrating the importance of gender-responsive service delivery approaches.

#### 10. Enrollment Without Learning: Teacher Effort, Knowledge, and Skill in Primary Schools in Africa (2017)
**Authors:** Bold, T., Filmer, D., Martin, G., Molina, E., Stacy, B., Rockmore, C., Svensson, J., & Wane, W.
**Journal:** Journal of Economic Perspectives, 31(4), 185-204

**Abstract:**
School enrollment has universally increased in low-income countries, but enrolling in school does not assure that children learn. This paper uses data from seven sub-Saharan African countries to answer: How much do teachers teach? What do teachers know? How well do teachers teach? The findings reveal significant challenges in teacher quality and effort.

#### 11. Community monitoring interventions to curb corruption and increase access and quality in service delivery: A systematic review (2017)
**Authors:** Molina, E., Carella, L., Pacheco, A., Cruces, G., & Gasparini, L.
**Journal:** Journal of Development Effectiveness, 9(4), 462-499

**Abstract:**
This Campbell systematic review assesses the effectiveness of community monitoring interventions in reducing corruption. The review summarizes findings from 15 studies across Asia, Africa, and Latin America. Results show that community monitoring can reduce corruption and improve health service use, with beneficial effects on education outcomes as measured by test scores.

**Key Finding:** "Community monitoring interventions can work, and CMIs can reduce corruption. However, it is important to understand the details of the intervention and the context in which it was applied."

#### 12. Polarization in Latin America: Patterns and Links with Institutions and Conflict (2008)

## Key Themes and Expertise

### On AI in Education
Ezequiel Molina believes that AI should be viewed as a tool to enhance human expertise rather than replace it. His work emphasizes:
- Ethical and responsible AI implementation
- Practical applications that reduce administrative burden
- AI as a complement to, not replacement for, teacher expertise
- Evidence-based approaches to AI adoption
- Importance of context-specific adaptation

### On Teacher Professional Development
Key principles from Molina's work:
- Evidence-based observation and feedback are essential for improving teaching quality
- Behavioral science insights can enhance teacher coaching effectiveness
- Teacher policies must address system-level barriers, not just blame individual teachers
- Professional development should incorporate adult learning principles
- Sustained support and coaching produce better outcomes than one-time training

### On Teaching Quality
From the Teach and Coach programs:
- Teaching quality can be measured reliably across diverse contexts
- Higher teaching quality correlates with better student outcomes
- Both cognitive and socio-emotional skills development matter
- Inclusive teaching practices are measurable and improvable
- Time on task and quality of instruction are both important

### On Education Technology
- Technology should serve pedagogical goals, not replace them
- Screen time in early childhood requires careful balance
- One-to-one device programs (like Ceibal) can succeed with proper implementation
- EdTech adoption requires teacher training and support
- Digital tools should reduce burden, not add complexity

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What is the Teach tool and how does it work?**
A: Teach is a free, open-source classroom observation tool that measures teaching quality through direct classroom observation. It captures both time on learning and quality of teaching practices across cognitive and socio-emotional domains. Observers use a standardized rubric to score teachers, providing reliable data for feedback and improvement.

**Q: How can AI help teachers?**
A: According to Molina's work, AI can help teachers by: reducing administrative paperwork (like El Director LIBRE does for principals), providing personalized feedback on teaching practices, offering adaptive learning support for students, and freeing up time for teachers to focus on instruction and student relationships.

**Q: What makes teacher professional development effective?**
A: Based on the Coach program and Making Teacher Policy Work, effective professional development includes: ongoing coaching rather than one-time workshops, observation-based feedback using tools like Teach, incorporation of behavioral science insights, adaptation to local contexts, and system-level support for implementation.

**Q: What is the global learning crisis?**
A: As documented in "Enrollment Without Learning," the global learning crisis refers to the phenomenon where school enrollment has increased dramatically, but many children complete primary school without basic literacy and numeracy skills. Molina's work shows this is often due to gaps in teaching quality and teacher support systems.

**Q: How should countries approach AI in education?**
A: Based on "AI Revolution in Education," countries should: start with clear pedagogical goals, ensure ethical implementation, train teachers to use AI tools effectively, adapt solutions to local contexts, view AI as enhancing rather than replacing teachers, and build evidence through rigorous evaluation.
